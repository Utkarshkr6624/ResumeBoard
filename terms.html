What Resumeboard is, and what it is not

Resumeboard is a resume builder written in plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. It lives at https://resumeboard.app, and the files your browser downloads are the whole application. There is no build step behind them, no package manager, and no process that runs when you press a key.

Utkarsh wrote it and operates it. He is an individual, not a registered company, and these terms are between you and him. The about page explains the design decisions behind the thing you are agreeing to use.

What it gives you

  • An editor where the page itself is the editing surface, so what you see while you type is what prints.
  • A set of templates you can switch between at any time.
  • Exports to PDF and to plain text, plus a JSON file you can keep as a backup.
  • Writing guides and an ATS reference, both static pages in the same folder.

No account, no server, no sync

None of those three exists. There is no sign-up, no login, no profile, no email address collected, and no database behind the page. Your resume is written to storage inside your own browser and is never transmitted.

The consequence is the one that decides this whole page. The app cannot sync between your devices, cannot recover a file it has lost, and cannot restore anything on request, because there is no copy of your work anywhere it could reach. What is on one device stays on that device unless you move it yourself, with the export file. The privacy page sets out exactly where the data sits and what the browser can do to it.

Third-party services

There is exactly one external service in the whole codebase, and it only runs if you ask it to.

cdnjs, on the PDF import path only

Reading a PDF needs a real PDF engine, and this project ships none. When you choose Import, then PDF, then pick a file, the app adds a script tag pointing at a version-pinned copy of pdf.js on cdnjs. Before anything is fetched, the import panel shows you both addresses, and the cancel keeps working while the download is in flight.

These are the exact URLs, taken from the constants at the top of assets/js/ui/importer.js:

https://cdnjs.cloudflare.com/ajax/libs/pdf.js/3.11.174/pdf.min.js https://cdnjs.cloudflare.com/ajax/libs/pdf.js/3.11.174/pdf.worker.min.js

The version is pinned rather than tracked, so the file you receive is the file anyone receives that day. The script tag carries crossorigin="anonymous" and referrerpolicy="no-referrer", which means the request carries no cookies and nothing about the page you came from.

Once it loads, the library runs inside your browser and is handed the bytes of the file you picked. Your PDF is not uploaded. Nothing about it reaches cdnjs or anyone else: not the file, not its name, not its size, not the text inside it. cdnjs sees one request for two public JavaScript files, in the same way it sees a request from any other site on the web. If you never use the PDF option, the request is never made.

The other three import routes — a .docx file, a .json file, and pasted text — are unpacked in the browser and make no request at all.

pdf.js is not ours and stays under whatever licence it is published under on the page it comes from. If you reuse any part of it, read that licence yourself at the addresses above rather than taking this page's word for it.

Your responsibilities, and what this service does not do

You are responsible for your document: what is in it, whether it is true, where you send it, and whether the file you exported is the one you meant to send. We have no way to check any of that, and we would not check it if we could.

  • The importer is a heuristic, not a model. A PDF or a .docx gets you close, not correct. Read the result before you send it.
  • Nothing here is a review, a score, or a guarantee. The app flags empty fields and thin sections as you type. It does not tell you whether a claim will hold up in an interview, and it does not know what a particular employer or applicant tracking system will make of the result.

  • You are responsible for your own backups. The app makes a JSON export in two clicks and then keeps no copy of it anywhere.

Features change. We may fix bugs, redesign the editor, or remove something you rely on, and we do not owe you a new version of a page you liked. There is no deprecation schedule and no frozen build.

No account, no recovery, no availability guarantee

Three things an online service normally provides are all missing here, on purpose. It is worth being blunt about what that costs.

No account

Nothing identifies you. There is no profile, no password, no sign-in history, no record of who typed what, and no way to prove later that you were the one who wrote a given document.

No recovery

If the data is gone, it is gone. A browser may evict site data when a device is low on space, clearing your browsing data removes it, a private window discards it on close, and a different browser, a different profile, or a different address is a different storage bucket that starts empty.

There is no backup held for you and nothing to request, because a deletion here is complete: there is no copy on any server. Export the JSON file and keep it somewhere you control. The export steps are on the privacy page.

No availability guarantee

Resumeboard is offered as it is. There is no uptime commitment, no service level, no response-time promise, and no support desk that is awake. Pages can change or be taken down. The builder can break in a browser update. Files can move, and a link you saved in an old resume or bookmark may stop resolving. Plan around that rather than around a promise we have not made.
