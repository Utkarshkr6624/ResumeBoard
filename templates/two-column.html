Two column with sidebar

The first two-column preset in the set, and one the builder is candid about: a narrow tinted sidebar carries the things a reader looks up, and the wider column carries the things a reader reads. It is a good page on A4 and a bad one to send to a parser.

The numbers

Every figure below is a value in the template registry the builder ships (assets/js/render/templates.js), or a size derived from it by the same multipliers the document stylesheet uses. Body text is not the preset’s nominal point size: it is that figure corrected for the face’s optical size, which is why a 10 pt Helvetica preset renders at 11.73 pt here. Nothing on this page is a measured pass rate or a hiring outcome, because neither exists.

What it is for

Good at

  • On screen, on A4, in a PDF sent to a person. This is a page that looks considered.
  • A lot of short factual items. Contact, skills, languages and certifications are reference material, and a column is the right shape for reference material.
  • Portfolio-adjacent roles where a little colour and a little structure are part of the pitch.
  • A conversation rather than an application: emailing a resume to a founder who asked for it.

Bad at

  • Applicant tracking systems. A columned layout is named on the published failure lists, and the sidebar is where a parser most often loses the contact block.
  • Print in the United States. The preset is A4 by default, and Letter is a different shape with a different amount of room.
  • Anywhere the document has to be pasted into a form, which flattens columns and reorders sections.
  • A long career. The main column is narrow and the sidebar is not, so the roles have the least room of any preset here.

ATS profile

Visual only

Not recommended for a parsed application. Greenhouse names a columned layout directly, and the reason is mechanical: a parser walking the document top to bottom reads the sidebar first and the roles second, so the section it thinks is the summary is the one with your email address in it.

The Taleo notes make the narrower version of the same point. A column is fine on screen and ambiguous in a text extraction order.

Use it when a person is the reader. If you do not know, assume a parser is somewhere in the chain and pick a one-column preset: classic or print-safe both stay one column.

Still yours to get right

A clean layout with a placeholder employer named “Company 1” is still a document a parser discards. No template can fix a sentence that describes effort instead of effect. Start with the resume basics guide and the one on quantifying impact.
